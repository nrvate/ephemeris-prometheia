# Eclipses

## Lunar eclipses

`include/prometheia/lunar_eclipse.hpp`, `src/lunar_eclipse.cpp`; the C API
is in [C_API.md](C_API.md), the Python in [PYTHON.md](PYTHON.md).

### Method

1. **Candidates.** For each lunation the true opposition (the Moon's
   apparent ecliptic longitude 180 degrees from the Sun's) is found by Newton
   from the mean full moon. If the Moon is then more than 2.4 degrees from
   the shadow axis, no eclipse is possible (the largest penumbra radius plus
   the Moon's semi-diameter is under 2 degrees); the rest are examined.
2. **Greatest eclipse.** The time of least angular distance between the
   Moon's centre and the shadow axis, which points from the geocentre away
   from the geocentric apparent Sun, found by a golden-section search and
   three parabolic refinements, in TT. Both bodies come from `Engine::calc`
   (geocentric, apparent, true equator and ecliptic of date), their
   Cartesian vectors; the angle is the arctangent of cross over dot, never
   an arccosine.
3. **Shadow radii.** Danjon's, the convention of NASA's canon:
   `Rp = 1.01 pi + s + p` for the penumbra and `Ru = 1.01 pi - s + p` for the
   umbra, with `pi = asin(Re / r_moon)` the Moon's horizontal parallax,
   `s = asin(Rsun / r_sun)` the Sun's semi-diameter and
   `p = asin(Re / r_sun)` the Sun's parallax. The Moon's semi-diameter is
   `m = asin(Rmoon / r_moon)`.
4. **Magnitudes.** With `d` the Moon's distance from the axis, umbral
   magnitude `(Ru + m - d) / 2m` and penumbral magnitude `(Rp + m - d) / 2m`.
   The umbral one is negative for an eclipse that misses the umbra, as in
   the canon. The class is total if the umbral magnitude is at least 1,
   partial if it is positive, else penumbral; no eclipse when the penumbral
   magnitude is not positive.
5. **Contacts.** P1/P4 are where `d = Rp + m`, U1/U4 where `d = Ru + m`,
   U2/U3 where `d = Ru - m`, each by the Illinois method to 1e-8 day, with the
   radii of that instant. U1/U4 exist when the umbral magnitude is positive,
   U2/U3 when it is at least 1; an absent contact is NaN (C) or `None`
   (Python).
6. **Gamma.** The Moon's distance from the axis at greatest eclipse,
   `r_moon sin d / Re`, in Earth equatorial radii; positive when the Moon is
   north (ecliptic latitude of Moon plus Sun positive).
7. **Time scales.** Everything is computed in TT. UT1 is TT less
   `Engine::delta_t_seconds`, the Delta T model the engine is using
   (the observed model by default, or one set with `set_delta_t_model`).
8. **Visibility.** `local` / `prometheia_lunar_eclipse_at` computes the
   Moon's topocentric apparent equatorial position at each contact and the
   maximum and its horizon altitude, true and with the standard
   atmosphere's refraction (1013.25 hPa, 15 C; `horizontal_ut`).

### Saros

Van den Bergh's (1955) inex-saros numbering. An eclipse's series follows
from its lunation number `L = floor((JD_TT - 2451550.09766) / 29.530588861)`:

    L = 135 s + 208  (mod 223),     s = 38 (L - 208) mod 223,   s in [-20, 202]

(`135 * 38 = 1 mod 223`). The **reference** pinning the numbering is
the 2026 March 3 total lunar eclipse, saros 133 in NASA's Five Millennium
Catalog of Lunar Eclipses (Espenak and Meeus, NASA/TP-2009-214173, catalog
number 09708). The relation holds for all 12,064 eclipses of that catalog
(-1999 to +3000; series numbers -20 to 183), checked by
`tools/gen/gen_lunar_saros.py`'s arithmetic; the interval between members
of a series is always exactly 223 lunations.

The **member** number counts from the series' first eclipse, a convention.
`src/lunar_saros.inc` tabulates the lunation of the first eclipse of the 162
series that begin inside the catalog (generated by
`tools/gen/gen_lunar_saros.py` from the catalog fetched by
`tools/fetch/eclipse_fetch.py`, sha256 pinned there); member is 0 for a
series not in the table (one already running in the year -1999 or starting
after 3000). Credit: "Eclipse Predictions by Fred Espenak, NASA's GSFC"
(public domain).

### Validation

All figures are measured, `DE440`, Delta T the engine's default.

**NASA's canon, 1900-2100** (`PROMETHEIA_LUNAR_CANON=<5MKLEcatalog.txt>
build/test_lunar_eclipse`; fetch with `tools/fetch/eclipse_fetch.py`). The
canon is in TD (it used VSOP87/ELP and its own Delta T), so the times are
compared in TT (TD):

| | measured |
|---|---|
| eclipses in the canon / found / extra | 459 / 459 / 0 |
| class (total/partial/penumbral) differing | 0 |
| saros series differing | 0 |
| greatest eclipse, worst difference | 2.73 s |
| umbral magnitude, worst difference | 0.0007 |
| penumbral magnitude, worst difference | 0.0007 |
| gamma, worst difference | 0.0001 |

The test takes the target of 5 s and 0.002 as its thresholds. The 459
eclipses with all contacts take 0.4 s.

**swetest** (DE440, printed output only; 457 eclipses from 1900-01-01 to
2100, compared in TT using swetest's printed `dt`, which is rounded to
0.1 s):

| | measured |
|---|---|
| kind, saros series, saros member differing | 0 of 457 |
| greatest eclipse, worst difference | 8.2 s |
| penumbral / umbral magnitude, worst difference | 0.0010 / 0.0011 |
| contacts: mean difference (U1..U4, P1/P4) | 2.4 s, 3.5 s |
| P1/P4 with penumbral magnitude above 0.3, worst | 8.2 s |
| U1/U4 with umbral magnitude above 0.1, worst | 7.3 s |
| U2/U3, worst (near-grazing totality) | 61 s |
| P contacts with penumbral magnitude below 0.1, worst | 125 s |

The two recorded eclipses (UT, swetest / ours):
2026-03-03, total: magnitudes 1.1507 / 2.1839 (canon 1.1507 / 2.1838), ours
1.1511 / 2.1842; maximum 11:33:41.2 swetest, contacts within 3.3 s of
swetest's six, saros 133/27 both. 2027-02-20, penumbral: 0.9266 swetest,
0.9266 canon, ours 0.9265; saros 143/18 both.

Contact times of a grazing contact are ill-conditioned (the Moon barely
moves across the circle), which is why the worst cases are the grazing ones
and why a difference in the shadow model shows there first. The model
difference to swetest in the ordinary contacts (about 3 s on average) was
not traced to a cause; the canon's greatest-eclipse times are matched
closer than swetest's.

### Limits

- The search covers 2000 lunations (about 160 years) before reporting not
  found; kinds that are rare (total only) are always within that.
- The shadow axis uses the apparent Sun; no refraction or atmosphere enters
  the contacts, the Danjon factor stands for the atmosphere.
- `jd_max` for `at` is matched within two days.
